Question 3 · why w follows h in ALiBi

One slope does two jobs

(a) Training sets the copy heads’ slope so the clock is right, so it grows with h (ρ = 0.93, 119 models).

(b) The same slope sets w (ρ = 0.84). Bayes keeps time and evidence apart; ALiBi ties them.

(c) Editing it in 50 unseen models: halve → w ×0.57; double → w ×1.33.

A real cause, but not the whole one: RoPE has no slope, yet its w also drops at very low h.

Why does w go up with h? For ALiBi models we found a mechanism. Each copy head has one learned number, its slope, which sets how fast its attention to a past copy fades with distance. Panel a: training sets that slope so the clock is right, so models trained with more switches get steeper slopes, very consistently. Panel b: the same slope also decides how much each extra recent copy tips the balance, so w rises with the slope. In Bayes, time and evidence are separate knobs; in an ALiBi head they are tied through one number. Panel c is the causal test. We took 50 trained models we had not used before and edited the slope by hand. Halving it made w fall to about 0.57 of its value, in almost every model. Doubling it raised w by about a third. We had pre-registered at least 30 percent for doubling; seed 0 gave 28 percent, a narrow miss, and seed 1 gave 37 percent. So this is a real causal channel. But it is not the whole story, because RoPE models, which have no slope at all, also give recent copies little weight at very low h.
