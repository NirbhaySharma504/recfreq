h′ = 1.45 · h^0.74

4 constants fitted to 12 settings: h from 0.003 to 0.03 (one decade), ε from 0.05 to 0.3. Nothing in Bayes predicts these numbers. So the real questions are: where does this summary hold, and why?

There are two formulas, and only one of them is derived. Prop. 2, the Bayes switch point, is derived, and we just corrected it. The law on this slide is not derived. It is a fit: we took each model's fitted beliefs h-prime and epsilon-prime, and fitted a power law and a straight line in logit space across 12 settings. Those 12 settings only cover one decade of h, 0.003 to 0.03, and typo rates from 0.05 to 0.3. So "is the law correct" really means "where does it describe the models, and why".
