Here is the full scorecard of what we predicted before seeing results in this round of work. Four predictions passed. A switch-point law, which says models get the time scale right but weigh evidence at about a third, predicted the switch points of the new models to within a factor of about 1.3. How often a conflict pattern occurs in ordinary training data predicts, on unseen models, how far the model deviates from Bayes on that pattern. The rise of w with h held on models we had not looked at. And slope surgery moved w in the predicted direction, with doubling just missing the size we set. Six predictions failed, and each failure taught us something: the clock is not the problem; extra conflict data helps but does not remove the deviation; context length, long gaps and training length do not set the low-h edge; RoPE is not immune; conflict data does not lift ALiBi at very low h; and the copy heads' attention is not where the copies get counted. We keep all of these in the record.
