Background · the ideal answer (Bayes)

Weigh "it really changed" against "the new copies are typos"

log-odds(old : new)  =  log( s / (1−s) )  +  log V  −  nw · log( (1−ε)/ε )
s = (1−h)G = chance the old value survived the gap
Time: the longer the gap, the more likely a switch happened.
Prior: a switch only helps the new value if the redraw lands on it, 1 time in V.
Evidence: each recent copy multiplies the odds by (1−ε)/ε, the odds it is clean rather than a typo (×9 at ε = 0.1).
Switch to the recent value when (small hG):
log(h·G) > log V − nw·log((1−ε)/ε)
→ old copies beyond 2 do not matter (nc cancels) · the gap enters as log G · each recent copy cuts the switch gap by (1−ε)/ε
Checked and corrected: the two-story logic is right, but our first version dropped three small terms (a redraw can land on the old value; old copies can be typos too; models predict the written token). With them, the formula matches the exact answer to 0.9%.
Bayes compares two stories. Story one: nothing changed, and the recent copies are typos. Story two: the value switched during the gap, and the recent copies are clean. The ratio gives three terms: time, which is how likely the old value survived the gap; the prior, because a switch only helps the new value if the redraw lands on it; and evidence, where each recent copy multiplies the odds by one minus epsilon over epsilon. The old copies appear in both stories in the same way, so they cancel, which is why Bayes does not care about more than two old copies. When we re-derived this for Sir, the logic held, but three small terms had been dropped; with them the formula matches the exact computation to under one percent.
