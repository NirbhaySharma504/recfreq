Error = formula's error ÷ the best Bayes fit to that same model. Inside its own fitting box the formula scores about ×1.4, so "works" means ×1.5 or better.
The band is tilted: higher h needs higher ε. (0.045, 0.2) works; (0.045, 0.1) and (0.03, 0.05) do not. * At h = 0.0003 switch points fall beyond our probe window.
The previous slide explained why the formula works inside the band. This one is the other side. At every edge of the band, the clock is still exact, and what changes is w. At high switch rates the slope is steep and w approaches one, so the believed typo rate drops toward the true one; the formula's typo law has no h in it, so it keeps predicting the in-band value and misses. At very low typo rates Bayes says each recent copy is very strong evidence, 2 to 4 nats, but the models give it much less. At very high typo rates it flips: Bayes says a recent copy is almost worthless, but the models still give it some weight and switch where Bayes never would; this regime barely affects the training loss, and two seeds with the same loss end up with very different w. The very low h edge is only partly explained; the scorecard on the next slide lists what we ruled out. The band is also tilted: at higher switch rates it needs a higher typo rate, which is why the high-h corner at low epsilon misses.
