Here is every prediction from the earlier rounds, each written down with a timestamp before we saw its result. Four passed: a switch-point law with the right clock and evidence at about a third predicted new models' switch points to within a factor of 1.3; how rare a conflict pattern is in training predicts how far models deviate from Bayes on it; w rises with h on models we had not looked at; and editing the ALiBi slope moves w in the predicted direction. Halving passed. Doubling raised w by 28 percent against the 30 percent we had registered, a narrow fail; the second seed, not separately registered, gave 37 percent. Six failed, and each taught us something: the clock is not the problem; extra conflict data helps but does not remove the deviation; context length, long gaps and training length do not set the low-h edge; RoPE is not immune; conflict data does not lift ALiBi at very low h; and the copy heads' attention is not where recent copies are counted. That last question is still open.
