Why are recent copies under-counted?

HypothesisVerdictWhat we saw
One ALiBi slope sets both the time term (clock) and the evidence weightSupported, partialEditing the slope moves w both ways (halve ×0.57, double ×1.33). But RoPE, with no slope, also under-weights at low h.
Conflicts are rare in training, so the weighing is poorly learnedPartialRarity predicts which cells deviate on unseen settings; extra conflict data lifts w only part of the way.
Context length, long-gap data or training length set the low-h edgeRefuted2× context, 9× long-gap data, 4× training: none moves it much.
Copy heads count recent copies by attention massRefutedMatching the mass reproduces only 11% of the extra copies' effect.
Still open: what fixes w at very low h (below 0.001). Where in the network recent copies are counted.
Four explanations we tested for why recent copies count too little. The ALiBi slope does tie the clock to the evidence weight: when we edit it by hand, the weight moves. But it cannot be the whole story, because RoPE has no such slope and shows the same drop at low h. The rarity of conflicts in training explains which situations deviate, but adding conflict data only helps part way. Two specific explanations were ruled out: neither context length, nor long-gap data, nor training length moves the low-h edge, and the copy heads do not count recent copies through their attention mass. What sets the evidence weight at the very lowest switch rates is still open.
