Robust to training settings; the evidence story replicates

Each bar: the formula's error after changing one training setting, divided by its error with the usual recipe (1.00 = no change). Learning rate, batch, training length and width 64 make it at most 7% worse. Width 256 sits right at our limit. MLPs or a 3rd layer push it past, so we claim only 2-layer attention-only models.
Slide 7's w graph again, now with the 72 new models (blue) over the earlier ones (grey). w climbs smoothly from ≈ 0.2 to 0.72 across the range, more cleanly than before. Its typical value (0.35) sits inside the band the formula's one fixed w stands for (≈ 0.25–0.65): the same reason as before for why the formula works here.
Left: changing the learning rate, batch size, training length or making the model narrower leaves the formula's error within 7 percent; the wider model misses our limit by half a percent. Changing the architecture does matter, so we only claim the formula for two-layer attention-only models. Right: we also measured the evidence weight on the new models. The trend from last time comes back more cleanly, and the typical weight sits in the range the typo-rate law was fitted on, which is the explanation for why the formula works inside this range and drifts outside it.
