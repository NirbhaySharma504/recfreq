Background · deriving the formula, 2 of 3

Divide the stories: time, prior and evidence

odds(old : new) = P(A) ÷ P(B) = s/(1 − s) · V · (ε/(1 − ε))nw
log-odds = log( s/(1 − s) ) + log V − nw · log( (1 − ε)/ε )
Time (the "clock"): how likely the old value survived the gap. Set by h and G only.
Prior: a switch only helps w if it lands on w, 1 time in V.
Evidence: each recent copy multiplies the odds by (1 − ε)/ε, ×9 at ε = 0.1. Set by ε and nw only.
Switch when log-odds < 0. For small hG, s/(1 − s) ≈ 1/(hG):
log(h·G) > log V − nw·log((1 − ε)/ε)
nc is gone: old copies say what the value was, not whether it changed.
Example, h = 0.01, ε = 0.1. Bayes switches to w after a gap of about:
200 pairs with one recent w · 55 with two · 8 with three.
Which story wins? Divide A by B. That is the odds that the old value is still true; above one keep c, below one switch to w. The old copies cancel. Taking logs splits the odds into three parts. Time, which we will call the clock: how likely the old value survived the gap, which depends only on h and the gap. The prior: a switch only helps w if it lands on w, one time in 64. Evidence: each recent copy multiplies the odds by one minus epsilon over epsilon, nine at epsilon 0.1, which depends only on epsilon and the number of recent copies. Setting the log-odds to zero gives the switch rule; for small h times G it is the familiar form log(hG) greater than log V minus n_w log((1 minus epsilon) over epsilon). For example at h 0.01 and epsilon 0.1, one recent w should be ignored until the gap is about 200 pairs, two win after about 55, three after about 8.
