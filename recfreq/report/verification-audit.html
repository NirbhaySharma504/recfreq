TOLLM project · verification audit

Verification audit of runs 1–5

Nirbhay Sharma, Gathik Jindal · 5 October 2026 · 155 trained models (123 from runs 1–5 + 32 audit runs), 36 unit tests, 31 recomputed headline numbers · companion to the run reports

Bottom line

The core results hold up. The code checks out, every headline number reproduces from the raw files, retraining reproduces the models to within a few percent, and the main effect survives new seeds, learning rates, batch size and null models. The audit found two overstated numbers, one wrong interpretation (the miscalibration is loss-optimal, not "cheap"), one pre-registered test reported more favourably than its own wording allows, and three citation errors, all corrected below and in the run reports.

3 · Are the results what they should be?

  • Every headline number reproduces. A claims registry recomputes 31 headline numbers (cliffs, switch-over gaps, learned slopes, win counts, laws, slope surgery, layout tests, scale tests, mechanism) from the per-run raw files with freshly written code: 31 of 31 match.
  • Theory, checked with independent code. The Prop. 2 boundary formula is within a median 10–15% of the exact Bayes switch-over gap (up to about 50% when h·G* approaches 3). Old copies beyond two change the exact log-odds by at most 0.11 nats from 2 to 16 copies. The per-copy weight log((1−ε)/ε) is exact to within 2% when h·G* ≤ 0.3, but overstates the exact weight by 30–55% when h·G* ≈ 1–3.
  • The fits are stable. A second optimizer on a finer grid gives the same h′ in 99% of models and the same ε′ in 100%. Bootstrap 90% intervals span a factor of 1.30 (h′) and 1.14 (ε′). h′ is significantly above the true h in 92% of models. Every fitted scale is positive.
Corrections to the run reports
  • "Models use about a third of the Bayes evidence per recent copy" overstated the gap. The 0.31 compared models with the theory value log((1−ε)/ε), which is too large wherever h·G* is not small. Against the exact Bayes weight on the same probes, models carry 0.24–1.06× of it (median 0.46×). Corrected to "about half".
  • Slope-surgery loss changes are +0.006 (×0.5) and +0.016 (×2) nats, not "+0.01 / +0.02". Corrected.
  • Slope surgery "monotone in all seeds" is 56 of 57 (see above). Corrected.
  • "The miscalibration is cheap, so training does not pin it down" was wrong. The finer sweep shows the trained slopes sit at the minimum of ordinary-data loss in every model, and calibrating would cost about 3× the excess loss. The miscalibration is loss-optimal for these heads. Corrected in the run reports.

5 · The concrete formulation

Prop. 2 · exact Bayes boundary

verified
switch to the recent value ⇔ log(h·G) > log V − n_w · log((1−ε)/ε) (small h·G; old copies beyond two irrelevant)

Exact switch-over gaps within a median 10–15% of the formula for h·G* ≤ 3; per-copy weight exact to 2% for h·G* ≤ 0.3.

Law 1 · trained models are Bayes with predictable wrong beliefs

predictive, R² 0.87
log p(c)/p(w) ≈ a + b · LO_Bayes(h′, ε′), h′ = 1.50 · h^0.74, logit ε′ = −0.33 + 0.44 · logit ε

Fitted on 11 of 12 training settings and used to predict the 12th from its (h, ε) alone, it reproduces the trained models' 112-cell conflict behaviour at median R² 0.87 (pre-registered bar 0.75). Baselines: true Bayes with scale and offset fitted on the held-out models themselves 0.74, the mean surface 0.55, an average counter 0.25. Weakest held-out settings: (h 0.003, ε 0.3) 0.57 and (h 0.03, ε 0.05) 0.72. Scope: 2-layer ALiBi models; RoPE models are closer to calibrated.

Law 2 · the fading rate sets the believed switch rate

causal, k ≈ 1.1–1.3
h′ ∝ s^k, k = 1.29 median (IQR 0.82–2.40) over 85 ALiBi models; k ≈ 1.1–1.2 for 2-layer attention-only models

Scaling the ALiBi slopes of all late heads by s ∈ {0.25, …, 4}: log h′ is linear in log s (per-model R² median 0.93; pre-registered > 0.9 ✓), with median elasticity 1.29 (pre-registered 0.7–1.3: met by the median, narrowly). The spread is wide: MLP and 3-layer models have k ≈ 1.6–2.3, and 27 models hit the fit floor at the smallest scale; over the central scales ×0.5–×2 the median is 1.15 (IQR 0.76–1.67). Ordinary-data loss is lowest at the trained slope in 85 of 85 models (+0.023 / +0.006 / +0.010 / +0.055 nats at ×0.5 / ×0.71 / ×1.41 / ×2). Bringing h′ down to the true h needs about ×0.44 on the slopes and costs a median +0.044 nats (IQR 0.020–0.082), about three times the models' excess loss.

Mechanism · decision from measured attention

descriptive
logit(c) − logit(w) ≈ Σ_heads γ_h · (A_c^h − A_w^h) + const

Median R² 0.90–0.99 across attention-only architectures. What remains open is how each head's attention split A_c / A_w comes to be Bayes-shaped.

Scope of all four statements: one task family (16 keys, 64 values, 256 pairs, structured typos), 2–3 layers, width ≤ 256, 20–60k steps.

6 · Literature

  • 17 cited works verified against arXiv and venue pages (titles, authors, arXiv IDs, venues).
  • Corrected: Zhou, Tian & Diggavi (2410.05493) is titled "An Information-Theoretic Approach to Understanding Transformers' In-Context Learning of Variable-Order Markov Chains" (AISTATS 2026); Makkuva et al. (2402.04161) appeared at ICLR 2025 as "Attention with Markov: A Curious Case of Single-Layer Transformers"; Rajaraman et al.'s NeurIPS 2024 venue is not confirmed on arXiv and should be checked before citing.
  • Novelty: no prior work found on conflicting old-versus-recent continuations with a Bayes switch-and-typo trade-off for induction heads. The two closest new papers found (copy-head phase transitions, 2606.12058; Markov-chain estimation in context, 2508.03934) do not overlap.

Remaining limitations

  • One synthetic task family; 2–3 layer models up to width 256.
  • Loss had not fully converged in some runs; behaviour was stable under 3× longer training.
  • h′ is weakly determined in settings whose switch-over lies beyond the probed gaps.
  • The mechanism explains where the decision is made and what sets the miscalibration, not how the attention split becomes Bayes-shaped.
  • No pretrained-LLM transfer test.
