Layer 2 · induction head: at the second A, attend to the token whose predecessor was A
K-composition. The previous-token head writes “my predecessor was A” into B’s residual stream; the induction head’s keys read that signal.
One offset only. In this picture a single offset −1 head supplies all the context the match can use.

Problem statement
In attention-only transformers deeper than two layers, do induction circuits compose a family of shift heads at offsets −1, −2, −3? … and does that composition let them match longer contexts, route around local noise, and prefer frequent-but-distant evidence over rare-but-recent evidence?
Scope: synthetic token-ID tasks · attention-only models of depth 0–4 · mechanism first, real-text checks later
Research questions
Four questions, one hypothesis each
RQ1 · LENGTH · INTERESTING QUESTION
Does a longer context (bigram → quadgram) need more composed offsets, and so more depth?
Hypothesis. 1–2 layer models solve bigrams but degrade on quadgrams; 3–4 layer models hold up.
RQ2 · LOCAL NOISE
If a token is inserted into the context, can the model route around the shifted offset?
Hypothesis. Deeper models show a smaller clean-vs-insertion accuracy gap than shallow ones.
RQ3 · REDUNDANCY
Is induction carried by one critical −1 head, or spread across several shift heads?
Hypothesis. Ablating one head gives partial, spread-out drops, not a collapse, in deeper models.
RQ4 · FREQUENCY VS RECENCY · LEAD QUESTION
Frequent-but-far correct completion vs rare-but-near typo: which one does the model copy?
Hypothesis. Choices follow log(n_c/n_t) > f(d_C) − f(d_B); depth reshapes f.

Theory · RQ4
A provable baseline for frequency vs recency
Setup. One idealised induction head. Every earlier match of the prefix gets the same score s, minus a positional penalty f(d) at distance d; the OV circuit copies the attended token.
Proposition. With n_c correct matches at distance d_C and n_t typo matches at distance d_B, the head puts more attention on the correct token if and only if
log(n_c / n_t) > f(d_C) − f(d_B)
Proof sketch. Softmax mass on each token is n·e^(s − f(d)) over a shared normaliser; s and the normaliser cancel in the ratio; take logs.
With ALiBi
f(d) = λd, so the boundary is exactly λ·(d_C − d_B).
In trained models
Our model learns its positions, so we measure f and ask how far, and why, it strays from the ideal.
Extension to test
A corrupted typo prefix costs βδ, shifting the right side to f(d_C) − f(d_B) − βδ.

Thread 1 of 6 · Foundations
Where induction heads come from
Elhage et al. · 2021 · Transformer Circuits Thread
A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits
Attention-only models decompose into QK and OV circuits. Two-layer models form induction heads by K-composing with a previous-token head that attends to offset −1.
For us: our model, K-composition score and ablation set-up come from here; we test whether one −1 head is the whole story beyond two layers.
Olsson et al. · 2022 · Transformer Circuits Thread
In-context Learning and Induction Heads
Induction heads form in an abrupt phase change that coincides with a jump in in-context learning, across model sizes; the authors argue they drive much of in-context learning.
For us: motivates studying induction in deeper models, where the circuit may be richer than the two-layer picture.

Thread 2 of 6 · Formation
How induction circuits are learned
Bietti et al. · 2023 · NeurIPS
Birth of a Transformer: A Memory Viewpoint
A two-layer model learns global bigram statistics first and in-context induction later; its weight matrices act as associative memories.
For us: separates global from in-context statistics; our typo task probes a finer split inside the context, frequent vs recent.
Singh et al. · 2024 · ICML
What Needs to Go Right for an Induction Head? A Mechanistic Study of In-Context Learning Circuits and Their Formation
Clamping activations during training reveals three interacting subcircuits behind induction-head formation; the heads that form are diverse and additive.
For us: additivity is the redundancy RQ3 looks for; clamping could test whether shift heads are needed for induction to form.

Thread 3 of 6 · Higher-order and statistical induction
Beyond one-token matching
Akyürek et al. · 2024 · ICML
In-Context Language Learning: Architectures and Algorithms
Transformers beat other architectures at in-context regular-language learning, using “n-gram heads”.
For us: our order-n triggers are n-gram heads in miniature; we ask how they are built.
Edelman et al. · 2024 · NeurIPS
The Evolution of Statistical Induction Heads
On in-context Markov chains, models move through uniform, unigram and bigram phases, ending with heads that estimate bigram counts.
For us: counting is frequency, not recency; our typo task sets the two against each other.
Chen et al. · 2024 · NeurIPS
Unveiling Induction Heads: Provable Training Dynamics
On n-gram Markov data, layer 1 copies a window of past tokens, an MLP selects the relevant ones, layer 2 matches.
For us: multi-offset copying can sit in one layer plus an MLP; ours has no MLP.

Thread 4 of 6 · Depth and structure selection
How much depth does induction need?
Rajaraman et al. · 2024 · NeurIPS
Transformers on Markov Data: Constant Depth Suffices
Three single-head layers can represent the in-context estimator for any k-th-order Markov source; attention-only builds use O(log k) layers.
For us: gives a concrete depth prediction for our attention-only models (RQ1).
Sanford, Hsu & Telgarsky · 2024 · ICML
Transformers, Parallel Computation, and Logarithmic Depth
Multi-hop (k-hop) induction is solvable with depth logarithmic in k; they argue shallower models cannot solve it.
For us: precedent that depth, not only width, can be the bottleneck.
d’Angelo, Croce & Flammarion · 2025 · ICLR
Selective Induction Heads
On interleaved Markov chains with different lags, a 3-layer model learns to identify the lag and copy from it.
For us: closest analogue to our mixed dataset: choosing which offset to trust (RQ2).

Thread 5 of 6 · Redundancy and self-repair
Ablations are not the whole truth
Wang et al. · 2023 · ICLR
Interpretability in the Wild (IOI circuit in GPT-2 small)
The circuit includes “backup” name-mover heads that take over when the main ones are ablated.
For us: backup heads are real in trained models; our ablations should expect them.
McGrath et al. · 2023 · arXiv
The Hydra Effect: Emergent Self-repair
Ablating one attention layer makes later layers compensate, so single ablations understate importance.
For us: a small drop may be repair, not designed redundancy (RQ3).
Rushing & Nanda · 2024 · ICML
Explorations of Self-Repair in Language Models
Self-repair appears across model families and sizes, but it is imperfect and noisy across prompts.
For us: compare zero, mean and path-level ablations before reading RQ3.

Thread 6 of 6 · Recency and ablation in pretrained LLMs
Induction heads carry temporal bias
Crosbie & Shutova · 2025 · NAACL Findings
Induction Heads as an Essential Mechanism for Pattern Matching in ICL
Ablating induction heads in pretrained LLMs cuts abstract pattern-task accuracy by up to ~32% and hurts few-shot tasks.
For us: head ablation matters at scale; our toy models ask which heads carry the match.
Mistry et al. · 2025 · NAACL
Emergence of Episodic Memory in Transformers
During training, attention develops human-like temporal patterns (contiguity, primacy, recency), driven by induction heads.
For us: recency is a learned bias; RQ4 asks when frequency should beat it.
Bajaj et al. · 2025 · arXiv
Beyond Semantics: How Temporal Biases Shape Retrieval
Retrieval over random tokens is U-shaped by position (start and end win); induction heads drive it in Transformers.
For us: a positional bias f(d) exists in LLMs; our proposition makes it measurable.

Threats to validity
Four fixes before the boundary experiment
1 · PILOT FIRST
A reported run of the typo notebook got only about 11–14% right at every depth.
Fix. Beat both baselines at every depth before any boundary sweep.
2 · DECOUPLE COUNTS AND DISTANCES
Correct is always frequent and far, typo always rare and near: counting and recency are confounded.
Fix. Vary n_c, n_t, d_C, d_B independently, including near-correct, far-typo cases.
3 · SWITCH-RATE EXPERIMENT
Train with a controlled rate h at which the true completion switches.
Test. Does the learned λ track the ideal forgetting rate λ*(h)?
4 · OPTIMISATION VS CAPACITY
In small toy models, optimisation failure can look like a capacity limit.
Fix. Sweep learning rates and seeds before reading any depth effect.

Evaluation criteria
What would count as evidence
Supports multi-offset composition
  • Early heads cluster at distinct fixed offsets (−1, −2, −3)
  • Several early heads K-compose with the same induction head
  • Single-head ablations cause partial, distributed drops
  • Deeper models keep quadgram and insertion accuracy near clean
Supports the single −1 head story
  • One early head sits at offset −1; the others are diffuse
  • Only that head K-composes strongly with the induction head
  • Ablating it collapses accuracy on clean and noisy sets alike
  • Depth adds no gain beyond two layers on quadgrams or typos
Either outcome is worth writing up: a clear negative result sharpens the two-layer picture.
