Follow-up 3b · why the evidence weight follows h

One ALiBi slope does two jobs: the clock and the evidence weight

(a) Clock → slope. How fast a copy head's attention decays with distance is learned to match how fast values change. Its slope grows with h across 75 models.
(b, c) Slope → evidence. The same slope sets how much each extra recent copy tips the balance. Editing it in 25 unseen models moves w: ×0.64 when halved (20/20 models), ×1.28 when doubled (14/16). This was pre-registered: the halving test passed, and the doubling test just missed its ×1.3 bound.
But not the whole cause: RoPE has no slope, yet its w also falls at very low h (0.23 at h = 0.0003). Where weighing old against new rarely matters in training, both encodings give recent copies little weight. ALiBi's slope tie makes it worse. Extra conflict data (orange) raises w.
This is the explanation for why the evidence weight depends on h. In an ALiBi copy head, a single learned slope sets how fast attention to an old copy decays with distance. Training tunes that slope so that a single old memory fades exactly as fast as values really change, which is why the clock is calibrated, and so the slope grows with h. But the same slope also decides how much each extra recent copy shifts the balance between old and new. Across our models the evidence weight tracks the slope, and when we edit the slope by hand in 25 models we had not used before, the evidence weight moves with it: down by a third when we halve it, up by about a quarter when we double it. We had predicted at least 30 percent for doubling, so that half just missed. Bayes treats time and evidence separately; ALiBi ties them through one number. So in ALiBi the slope is one real cause of the drift. But it is not the whole story: RoPE models, which have no such slope, also give recent copies little weight at very low h. Our reading is that the weight is low wherever weighing old against new rarely matters for the training loss, and that ALiBi's slope makes this worse. Training with extra conflict examples raises the weight, which fits that reading.
