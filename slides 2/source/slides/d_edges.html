One picture for inside and outside: time is handled like Bayes everywhere. Evidence is handled as a compressed, h-dependent weight. The law works where that weight sits near its in-box value, and fails exactly where it moves.
Sir asked why the formula fails outside the region. At every edge the clock is still right, and what moves is the weight given to recent copies. At high h, recent copies get almost the full Bayes weight, so the believed typo rate drops, but the formula has no h in its typo part. At very high typo rates, the models still give each recent copy about half a nat or more, while Bayes gives almost nothing, so the models switch far harder than any Bayes curve. At very low typo rates it is the other way round, and the misfit is mild. At very low h the picture is less clean: a single memory is still trusted correctly far beyond the gaps seen in training, but recent copies only count once the old copies are far away. Doubling the context length changed the switch point only slightly, so that is not the cause. We are testing whether it is the amount of long-gap training data, by changing the number of keys.
